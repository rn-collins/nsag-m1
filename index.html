Trauma-Informed Legal Space

12 questions  ·  6 dimensions  ·  8–10 minutes

Legal spaces are among the most consistently activating environments in civic life. A legal space that activates the threat response is a legal space that compromises due process. Trauma-informed design is not a courtesy — it is a legal and ethical obligation for any institution claiming to deliver justice.

Question 1 of 12

This assessment scores your organization across six dimensions. Each question uses a five-point scale. Your tier report is generated immediately after you submit your email — no waiting, no sales call required.

For: Legal aid · Public defenders · Courthouse administrators · Law school clinics · Restorative justice programs

Q1 of 12
Physical Environment Design

Does your space have documented standards for lighting, acoustics, and seating that distinguish client-facing areas from staff areas — based on reducing physiological activation?

Q2 of 12
Physical Environment Design

Has your facility been assessed for features that activate threat response — including exposed waiting areas, inadequate privacy, hostile acoustic environments, or poor lighting?

Q3 of 12
Procedural Design & Plain Language

Are all standard client-facing documents, forms, and notices written at or below a 6th-grade reading level and available in plain language?

Q4 of 12
Procedural Design & Plain Language

Do your intake and orientation procedures explicitly explain what will happen, in what order, and what the client's rights are — before any substantive interaction begins?

Q5 of 12
Interpreter & Translation Access

Does your organization have a formal protocol for identifying language access needs at first contact — before the client must self-identify or request an interpreter?

Q6 of 12
Interpreter & Translation Access

Are professionally qualified interpreters (not family members or bilingual staff pressed into service) available for all languages represented in your client population?

Q7 of 12
Staff Training & Trauma-Informed Behavior

Have all client-facing staff received trauma-informed care training within the past two years — including content on secondary trauma, nervous system activation, and co-regulation?

Q8 of 12
Staff Training & Trauma-Informed Behavior

Does your organization have a protocol for de-escalation that does not rely on punitive, coercive, or authority-asserting responses as a first intervention?

Q9 of 12
Access & Accommodation Infrastructure

Does your facility have a formal process for identifying and accommodating neurodivergence, sensory sensitivities, and physical accessibility needs without requiring clients to repeatedly self-disclose?

Q10 of 12
Access & Accommodation Infrastructure

Can a client with a disability navigate your intake process, waiting environment, and consultation space without barriers that require staff assistance not available to non-disabled clients?

Q11 of 12
Feedback & Accountability Mechanisms

Does your organization have a formal mechanism for clients to report negative experiences — including environmental, procedural, and relational harms — that is genuinely separate from the case relationship?

Q12 of 12
Feedback & Accountability Mechanisms

Are client feedback data reviewed at regular intervals by leadership, and is there a documented process for translating feedback into organizational design changes?
